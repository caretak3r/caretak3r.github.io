HOLD

Rigaku has been building X-ray instruments since 1951 and still produces its semiconductor metrology tools through a single factory in Takatsuki, Osaka [fact] — 2,000 employees, one site, ¥94.2B in FY2025 revenue shipped through a plant that falls under Japan's Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act, the same export-control regime that constrains lithography equipment shipments to China [fact]. The X-ray tools measuring GAA gate dimensions and 3D NAND bond layers are, by regulatory designation, dual-use instruments [fact]. Onto Innovation's 27% equity stake — purchased from Carlyle's Atom Investment in April 2026, no capital reaching Rigaku — converts a pre-existing CD-SAXS collaboration into a commercial channel: Rigaku's X-ray stack paired with Onto's optical metrology and AI analytics, pushed through 500+ Onto field engineers into foundries Rigaku couldn't reach at scale alone [fact]. No competitor currently ships hybrid X-ray-optical metrology as an integrated offering [inferred]. The moat compounds slowly — foundry metrology qualifications lock in on node-to-node cycles measured in years, and the switching cost is requalification time, not price [inferred]. X-ray diffraction patterns don't requalify themselves for the next vendor.

Rigaku Holdings occupies a differentiated niche in X-ray analytical and metrology technologies, with a particularly attractive positioning in semiconductor process control for advanced nodes (GAA, 3D NAND, HBM). The Onto Innovation 27% equity investment and strategic alliance (April 20, 2026) is the most significant corporate event since the October 2024 IPO, validating Rigaku's X-ray technology stack and providing access to Onto's global sales channel and AI-driven analytics software. The semiconductor capex upcycle (TSMC $36-40B capex, Samsung GAA ramp, HBM capacity buildout) creates a multi-year demand tailwind for Rigaku's metrology tools. However, the investment case at current prices is undermined by four factors: (1) Valuation — 54x P/E on declining earnings with zero FCF provides no margin of safety; (2) The bull thesis fails 2 of 5 items on the Specificity Bar (catalysts lack quantitative thresholds, $300M TAM is self-claimed), capping the rating at Hold; (3) Carlyle/Atom Investment remains a significant shareholder with potential selling pressure; (4) OTC liquidity (avg. <10K shares/day) makes position management difficult for institutional allocators.

Quantitative Lane

Customer Concentration

Rigaku does not publicly disclose customer-level revenue breakdowns in English-language filings. The semiconductor segment serves major global foundries and IDMs — Samsung, TSMC, SK Hynix, Micron, and Kioxia are the primary customer base for X-ray metrology tools. The Onto Innovation alliance press release references 'global customer reach' via Onto's network, implicitly confirming that Rigaku's current semiconductor customer base is narrower than Onto's. Estimate: top 5 semiconductor customers likely represent 40-50% of the semiconductor segment revenue. The General Analytical segment is more diversified across academia, life science, and industrial customers across 136 countries.

Backlog & Book-to-Bill

Rigaku does not publicly disclose quarterly backlog or book-to-bill metrics in its English-language IR materials. The Q4 FY2025 revenue surge to ¥34.7B (vs ¥18.7B in Q3) suggests strong back-half bookings/deliveries. The company's expansion of production capacity at Takatsuki (announced May 2025) implies management expects sustained order flow. FY2025 H1 operating profit decline (Q2 OP ¥5.7B vs ¥8.7B YoY) may indicate order timing shifts rather than structural margin compression. Without explicit book-to-bill disclosure, the forward order trajectory remains opaque.

Risk Factors (Item 1A)

  1. Customer Concentration in Semiconductor Segment: The semiconductor process control segment relies on a limited number of large IDMs and foundries. Loss of a major customer could materially impact revenue. (Source: Rigaku Integrated Report 2025, rigaku-holdings.com/english/ir/reports/)
  2. Carlyle/Atom Investment Overhang: Atom Investment (Carlyle) remains a significant shareholder post-IPO. Future share sales could create downward price pressure. The Onto Innovation 27% purchase partially mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.
  3. Technology Obsolescence / Competitive Pressure: Competitors like Bruker, KLA Corporation, and Onto Innovation itself could develop overlapping X-ray or alternative metrology solutions.
  4. Foreign Exchange Exposure: With ~70% of revenue from overseas, JPY strengthening against USD/EUR/KRW could compress reported revenue and margins.
  5. Single-Source Manufacturing Risk: Semiconductor metrology instruments manufactured primarily at the Takatsuki (Osaka) facility. Any production disruption would directly impact delivery capability.
  6. Regulatory/Export Control Risk: Advanced semiconductor equipment may be subject to export controls (Japan's Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act), limiting market access to certain geographies.
  7. Capital Structure Risk: D/E of 70.3% is elevated. While current ratio (2.27x) provides short-term liquidity, high leverage constrains financial flexibility.
