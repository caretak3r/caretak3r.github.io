In 2001 Jordan Wu — MIT-trained, fresh out of a Trident Microsystems engineering tour — co-founded Himax in Tainan to build the silicon that drove Taiwan's nascent LCD-TV ramp. The company IPO'd in 2006, became dominant during the 2010-2012 flat-panel cycle, and then spent a decade as a forgotten name as display-driver ASPs bled out and the ticker drifted into the dollar-sign-and-a-yield bucket. The inflection was not a contract or an acquisition — it was a budget decision. Sometime around 2018 Wu started routing R&D toward LCoS microdisplays, ultra-low-power AI vision (WiseEye), and co-packaged optics for hyperscaler switches, holding R&D at 20.5% of sales against an industry norm of 8-12%. For eight years that line item looked like a margin drag. Today it looks like three optionality engines lighting up at once — a "leading brand" smart-glasses design-win for WiseEye, hundreds of automotive Tcon design-wins, and Gen 1 CPO commercial shipments in H2 2026. The market is pricing a 60x trailing P/E, 73% China revenue, and a forgotten ticker. The actual asset is a fabless balance sheet that has been buying multi-engine optionality at full freight while everyone else priced the LCD comp.
