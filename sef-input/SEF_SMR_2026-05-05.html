NuScale Power was spun out of Oregon State University in 2007 — DOE national-lab IP, a small reactor design intended to solve the one problem the AP1000 generation could not, which was that nobody could finance a $14B single-asset bet anymore. Fluor took a controlling stake in 2011 and carried the company through a decade of regulatory work that culminated in 2023 with the NRC certifying the 50 MWe design — the only SMR ever certified in the United States. Then the original commercial path broke. The Carbon Free Power Project, the UAMPS deployment that was supposed to be the reference plant, was cancelled in November 2023 when escalated cost pushed the LCOE past what muni offtakers would underwrite. What was left was a certified design with no customer.

The inflection arrived from outside the company: AI data-center load forecasts that quietly added a hyperscaler-scale dispatchable demand curve, a Trump-administration nuclear tailwind, and NRC approval of the upsized 77 MWe variant. The market now prices SMR as a binary on contract conversion — TVA's 6 GW non-binding MOU, the ENTRA1 commercialization vehicle, Fluor's full equity exit — against a $1.25B cash cushion and accelerating burn. Watch Q3 2026 for binding-contract velocity; that is the print that resolves the binary.

The bear's case is well-rehearsed but conflates execution risk with thesis risk. Let me address the strongest points.

If Fluor knew NuScale was impaired, they would have sold at $20+ in late 2024, not at $10-15 in 2025-2026 at the bottom of the drawdown.

On the "low bar" earnings critique: The bear says "a low bar can be missed." True. But the bar has already been reset — Q1 revenue collapse is known and priced. On dilution: The bear is right that dilution is severe. But mark-to-market the alternative: a venture-stage company without dilution would be a venture-stage company that dies. Bottom line: I am not arguing this is an obvious buy. I am arguing it is a small-position, high-asymmetry trade with a falsifiable thesis and a definable kill-switch. On budget cycle:

  • The Fluor exit framing as "Fluor's capital structure issue" is wishful thinking. Fluor was the prime engineering partner, the manufacturing partner, and the largest equity holder. On the kill-switch:

  • The 2027-06-30 trigger is well-formed but generous. By then, the company will have raised additional capital twice over (332M → 662M share authorization request makes this explicit). Even if a binding PPA is signed in 2027, the per-share economics will have been further diluted by 30-50%.

My direct counters:

  1. Customer concentration (current & forward) = catastrophic. 92% historical Fluor revenue → Fluor exited. ENTRA1 is a cost, not a customer. TVA is non-binding. RoPower FID slipped to 2027.

  4. Competitive reality: HSBC chose Oklo. ARK is rotating to X-energy private. Constellation's stock is up. The market is voting against this specific name.
  5. Going-concern adjacency: The DEF 14A explicitly references "substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern" if the share authorization vote fails. This is in the company's own disclosure.

My recommendation: Avoid. There are better ways to express the nuclear thesis (CEG, BWXT, even OKLO with its tighter cap structure). NuScale is a venture-stage company in a public-market wrapper — wrong instrument for the thesis.

My final recommendation stands: Avoid. If thematic exposure is required, get it via Constellation, Vistra, or BWXT. If pure-SMR exposure is required, get it via Oklo's tighter capital structure or an SMR ETF. NuScale specifically is the worst expression of a good thesis.

News & Macro

The macro and sector backdrop for NuScale is genuinely the best it has ever been — which makes the equity's 80% drawdown all the more diagnostic of company-specific issues.

Macro / Sector Tailwinds (Real)

  1. Trump administration nuclear policy: The current administration has explicitly prioritized nuclear restart and SMR deployment. The U.S. Department of Energy on December 2, 2025 announced an $800 million funding initiative to accelerate SMR deployment.
  2. Hyperscaler demand: Microsoft (Three Mile Island restart), Amazon (Talen Energy partnership), Google, Meta, and Oracle have all signed dispatchable-power deals with nuclear operators in the last 18 months. Data center power demand is forecast to grow 160% by 2030 (industry consensus).
  3. Grid reliability politics: NERC, FERC, and state PUCs are increasingly focused on baseload retirement risk, which structurally favors SMR.
  4. Defense / DoD interest: Project Pele and analogous initiatives have created a parallel defense-microreactor demand channel.
  5. Foreign policy: U.S. SMR exports — particularly the Romania (RoPower) project — are a stated State Department priority for energy-security counterweight to Russia/China.

Macro / Sector Headwinds (Also Real)

  1. Renewables + storage cost curve: Utility-scale solar + battery is now $30-50/MWh delivered in many markets. SMR economics depend on a >$80/MWh assumption that is not yet validated at scale anywhere.
  2. Interest rates: SMR is a long-duration capital-intensive cash flow — every 100 bps of rate is meaningful to NPV. The 10-yr has compressed but remains elevated relative to 2020-21 financing assumptions.
  3. Permitting / FOAK risk: Even with NRC design approval, site-specific licensing and first-of-a-kind construction risk has not been demonstrated commercially in the U.S. since the 1990s.

  • Constellation (CEG), Vistra (VST), Talen (TLN): The "buy existing nuclear" trade has worked — these names are up materially while NuScale is down. Capital is preferring proven megawatts.
